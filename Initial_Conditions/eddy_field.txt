========================================
SIMULATION GOAL
Many small vortices (eddy field) generation
========================================

----------------------------------------
INITIAL VORTICITY (copy & paste)
----------------------------------------

3.0*(cos(18*atan2(z,x)) + 0.7*cos(24*atan2(z,x) + 1.3) + 0.5*cos(30*atan2(z,x) - 0.7)) * exp(-18*(y-0.15)^2)


----------------------------------------
SHORT THEORETICAL EXPLANATION
----------------------------------------

This initial condition injects energy at relatively small spatial scales
(high zonal wavenumbers). The many cosine terms create strong vorticity
gradients inside a narrow mid-latitude band.

In a 2D rotating fluid, nonlinear advection stretches and rolls these
gradients, rapidly generating many small coherent vortices. This is a
classical mechanism in geophysical turbulence.

Because the flow is nearly two-dimensional, energy tends to move toward
larger scales (inverse energy cascade), while enstrophy moves toward
smaller scales. As a result:

• Initially: many small vortices appear.
• Later: vortices interact and merge.
• Eventually: fewer but larger coherent structures remain.

The narrow Gaussian band represents a storm-track region, where strong
shear and vorticity gradients favor the growth of small-scale eddies,
similar to what happens in the real atmosphere and oceans.


----------------------------------------
WHY THIS IS INTERESTING
----------------------------------------

• It clearly demonstrates the formation of many small vortices.
• It shows the transition from fine-scale turbulence to larger
  coherent structures.
• It is useful to study:
    - geophysical turbulence,
    - inverse cascade,
    - vortex interaction and merging,
    - mixing and transport.

In vorticity (ζ), you will see many intense small vortices and filaments.
In the stream function (ψ), you will observe many small closed contours
that later merge into larger circulation cells.


----------------------------------------
SUGGESTED PARAMETERS
----------------------------------------

Start with:
  viscosity (nu) = 2e-4 to 3e-4
  angular velocity = 0.2

If vortices are too weak:
  increase amplitude slightly.

If the simulation becomes unstable:
  increase viscosity.
