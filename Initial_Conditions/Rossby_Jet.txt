========================================
INITIAL CONDITION
(Highly visual Rossby–jet interaction pattern)
========================================

1.1*tanh(7*y) + 1.4*cos(4*atan2(z,x) + 2.2*sin(3*y))*exp(-10*y^2) + 0.9*cos(9*atan2(z,x) + 1.6*sin(6*y))*exp(-18*(y-0.35)^2)


========================================
THEORETICAL DESCRIPTION
========================================

This initial condition is designed to generate a visually rich and 
physically meaningful flow resembling large-scale atmospheric dynamics.
It combines a zonal jet, planetary-scale Rossby waves, and smaller-scale
localized disturbances.

1) Background jet (first term)
   The hyperbolic tangent creates a large-scale shear flow similar to a 
   mid-latitude jet stream. This provides a waveguide for Rossby waves 
   and supports barotropic instability.

2) Large-scale Rossby wave (second term)
   The wavenumber-4 cosine introduces a planetary-scale meander of the 
   jet. The nonlinear phase modulation produces smooth, realistic wave 
   shapes and large coherent structures.

3) Localized wave packet (third term)
   The higher-wavenumber component is confined in latitude. It promotes
   wave breaking, filament formation, and the generation of coherent 
   vortices as the flow evolves.

Together, these mechanisms reproduce the transition from smooth 
planetary waves to nonlinear turbulence.


========================================
WHY THIS INITIAL CONDITION IS INTERESTING
========================================

• It produces striking visual patterns:
    - large meandering jets,
    - coherent vortices,
    - thin filaments,
    - mixing regions.

• It clearly demonstrates:
    - Rossby wave propagation,
    - nonlinear wave–mean flow interaction,
    - wave breaking,
    - vortex formation,
    - transport and mixing.

• It is excellent for comparing:
    - vorticity (local structures and filaments),
    - stream function (global circulation),
    - contour plots (flow speed and transport barriers).

• The evolution naturally leads to inverse cascade behavior and the 
  emergence of large-scale coherent structures.


========================================
SUGGESTED PARAMETERS
========================================

For best visual results:
   viscosity (nu) ~ 3e-4 to 7e-4
   angular velocity ~ 0.15 to 0.25

Lower viscosity enhances filament formation and small-scale vortices.
Higher viscosity produces smoother and more stable large-scale patterns.

If the simulation becomes unstable:
   - slightly increase viscosity,
   - or reduce the amplitude of the cosine terms.
