========================================
INITIAL CONDITION
(Double jet interaction → large vortices and banded flow)
========================================

1.2*(tanh(8*(y-0.35)) - tanh(8*(y+0.1))) + 0.7*cos(6*atan2(z,x))*exp(-12*(y-0.1)^2)


========================================
THEORETICAL DESCRIPTION
========================================

This initial condition represents a simplified atmospheric circulation
with two jet streams interacting and a planetary-scale perturbation.

1) Double jet structure
   The difference of two tanh profiles creates two strong shear layers.
   These resemble multiple jet systems commonly found in planetary
   atmospheres such as Earth and Jupiter. These shear zones are
   dynamically unstable and act as waveguides for Rossby waves.

2) Planetary wave perturbation
   The cosine term introduces a large-scale Rossby wave with zonal
   wavenumber 6. The Gaussian envelope localizes the perturbation in
   latitude, so the wave interacts strongly with the jets.

As the simulation evolves, the wave grows and interacts with the jets.
This leads to deformation of the flow and the formation of coherent
vortices.

Over time, nonlinear effects generate:
   - vortex formation,
   - filamentation,
   - vortex merging,
   - emergence of large-scale structures.

This behavior is characteristic of barotropic geophysical turbulence.


========================================
WHY THIS INITIAL CONDITION IS INTERESTING
========================================

• It produces rich and visually striking dynamics.

• It demonstrates key physical mechanisms:
   - barotropic instability,
   - Rossby wave growth,
   - nonlinear wave–mean flow interaction,
   - inverse energy cascade,
   - formation of persistent large-scale vortices.

• It resembles phenomena observed in real atmospheres:
   - jet stream variability,
   - large planetary vortices,
   - banded atmospheric structures.

• In vorticity (w), you will observe:
   - strong vortices,
   - thin filaments,
   - vortex interactions.

• In the stream function (ψ), you will see:
   - banded flows,
   - closed circulation regions,
   - large-scale transport structures.

• Streamfunction contour lines highlight transport barriers and
  recirculation zones, which are essential in atmospheric dynamics.
