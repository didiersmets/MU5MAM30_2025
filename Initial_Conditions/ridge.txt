========================================
INITIAL CONDITION (Blocking event: ridge + split-jet deformation)
========================================

0.9*cos(3*atan2(z,x) + sin(2*y))*exp(-12*(y-0.25)^2) + 0.7*exp(-25*((x+0.3)^2 + (y-0.3)^2 + z^2))

========================================
THEORETICAL DESCRIPTION
========================================

This initial condition is a simplified model of an atmospheric blocking
configuration: a persistent large-scale ridge that disrupts the usual
zonal flow and forces the jet stream to meander strongly.

It is built from three components:

1) Large-scale ridge / planetary wave (first term)
   A smooth zonal wavenumber-3 disturbance is localized in latitude by a
   Gaussian envelope. This produces a strong, coherent meander that can
   behave like a blocking ridge.

2) High-pressure-like anomaly (second term)
   A localized positive vorticity anomaly acts as a coherent feature that
   can anchor or reinforce the ridge pattern.

3) Competing anomaly (third term)
   A nearby negative anomaly creates asymmetry and encourages deformation
   and reorganization of the flow, making the evolution more dynamic.

Together, these terms create a flow that can develop long-lived, slowly
evolving large-scale structures, while still allowing filament formation
and eddy shedding for sufficiently low viscosity.


========================================
WHY THIS INITIAL CONDITION IS INTERESTING
========================================

• Blocking is one of the most important large-scale atmospheric regimes:
  it can persist and strongly alter transport and mixing.

• In vorticity (ζ), you will see coherent anomalies interacting and
  shedding filaments.

• In stream function (ψ), you will see large closed contours and strong
  meanders: an intuitive signature of a blocked flow.

• The streamfunction contour lines clearly show transport barriers and
  recirculation regions (closed loops), which is exactly what makes
  blocking “persistent” and meteorologically impactful.

• This setup is excellent for comparing parameter effects:
    - lower viscosity → more filamentation and eddy shedding
    - higher viscosity → smoother, longer-lived, more laminar blocking
    - higher angular velocity → stronger advection/phase drift of the pattern


========================================
SUGGESTED PARAMETERS
========================================

For best results:
   viscosity (nu) ~ 1e-3 to 1e-2
   angular velocity ~ 0.05 to 0.2
