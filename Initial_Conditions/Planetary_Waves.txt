========================================
INITIAL CONDITION (Polar vortex + planetary wave)
========================================

2.0*exp(-25*(1 - y)^2) - 0.8*cos(2*atan2(z,x))*exp(-10*(1 - y)^2)


========================================
THEORETICAL DESCRIPTION
========================================

This initial condition represents a simplified model of a polar vortex 
disturbed by a large-scale planetary wave. It is inspired by real 
atmospheric dynamics in the stratosphere and high latitudes.

The structure contains two main components:

1) Polar vortex (first term)
   The exponential term creates a strong, localized region of vorticity 
   near the pole. This mimics the polar vortex, which is a large-scale 
   rotating circulation commonly observed in the Earth's atmosphere.

2) Planetary wave perturbation (second term)
   The cosine term introduces a large-scale longitudinal disturbance 
   with zonal wavenumber 2. This is similar to Rossby waves generated by 
   topography or land-sea temperature contrasts in the real atmosphere.

The perturbation deforms the vortex, causing it to shift, elongate, or 
split depending on the model parameters.


========================================
WHY THIS INITIAL CONDITION IS INTERESTING
========================================

• It reproduces key features of stratospheric dynamics, including 
  displacement and deformation of the polar vortex.

• It allows visualization of wave–vortex interaction, which is central 
  to understanding sudden stratospheric warmings.

• It produces large-scale coherent structures that remain visible over 
  long simulation times.

• It is numerically stable and well-suited for studying:
    - Rossby wave propagation at high latitudes
    - Vortex deformation and displacement
    - Wave–mean flow interaction
    - Large-scale atmospheric variability

• Depending on viscosity and angular velocity, the vortex may:
    - remain stable,
    - become elliptical,
    - split into multiple vortices,
    - or generate filament structures.


========================================
SUGGESTED PARAMETERS
========================================

For best results:
   viscosity (nu) ~ 1e-3 to 1e-2
   angular velocity ~ 0.05 to 0.2

It is also possible to play with the coefficient in the equation of the initial condition

Lower viscosity enhances vortex deformation and filament formation, 
while higher viscosity stabilizes the vortex.
